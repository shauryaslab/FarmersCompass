How to use Farmers Compass

A quick guide to planning and reading your results.

  1. Pick your field

    Click your field on the map, or type its latitude and longitude. Then choose the soil texture closest to yours (loam if you're unsure).

  2. Enter what you grow now

    Type your current rotation in order, separated by commas, e.g. corn, corn, barley. Use the crop names shown under the box.

  3. Choose the weather

    Recent climate tests against real seasons from 1995–2024. Around 2050 shifts them using NASA climate projections.

  4. Set priorities and simulate

    Move the sliders toward what matters most to you, then press Simulate. Results usually take a few seconds.

Reading the results

Score
How well the rotation fits your priorities. Higher is better. Rotations are listed best first.
Beats yours in
The share of weather futures in which this rotation scores higher than what you grow now. It is often 100% or 0%, because over 20 years the weather evens out.
Chips
The change against your current rotation. A filled green chip is better, an outlined red chip is worse.
Small grey range
The 10th to 90th percentile: in 8 of 10 weather futures the result falls in this range. The big number is the average.
Soil carbon
Carbon gained in the soil over 20 years (t C/ha), compared with growing annual crops only.
Water use
Water the crops need each year, in mm. Less is better.
Yield index
Average production per year, relative between crops.
+ cover crop
A winter cover crop is planted after each annual crop in the rotation.

Common questions

Where does the data come from?

Past weather from NASA POWER and Daymet, 2050 projections from NASA NEX-GDDP climate models, summer greenness from NASA MODIS satellites, and the 10-day forecast from NASA GEOS.

What is a "weather future"?

A possible 20-year sequence of growing seasons, made by reshuffling real past years at your field. Testing about 1,000 of them shows how each rotation holds up in good and bad runs of weather.

I got an "unknown crops" error.

Each crop in your current rotation must match one of the crop names listed under the box exactly, separated by commas.

Some tables say no data is saved for my location.

Greenness history and 2050 projections are downloaded ahead of time for each location. The message shows the command to fetch them; ask whoever runs the site to do it.

Do the forecast and greenness tables change the ranking?

No. They are shown for context about your field. Only the weather record, soil, crops and your priorities feed the ranking.

Should I change my rotation based on this alone?

Treat it as a planning aid. It's a simulation, so talk the top options through with an agronomist or Perennia before committing a season.
